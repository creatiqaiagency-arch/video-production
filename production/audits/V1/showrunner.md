# Showrunner audit — V1
SCORE: 5/10
VERDICT: V1 is a complete, coherent 6.5-minute film. The story works: hook, power, abuse of it, overload, the misunderstanding, the reveal, the lesson and the Maya payoff all land in order. The palette family (dusk magenta/purple, warm windows, black-head characters, DOF, grain, letterbox) is recognisably aimed at the Ifverse look. But side by side with the reference it reads as a flat, stage-like motion-graphics piece, not a cinematic animated short. Almost every set is a wall parallel to the lens. The exposure is blown (windows and skies clip to flat cream/white). Characters mostly hold poses while only their mouths and eyes change. Thought-window text is often too small, too faint or cropped to read. The emotional climax (Act 3, s12–s17, about 100 s) plays out in front of one identical blurred building façade, so the most important scene in the film looks the cheapest. I would not publish this on a million-subscriber channel yet. The script and structure are publishable. The picture needs a real camera, real light and real acting. Fix those three and the film can reach 7–8.

## TOP-10 changes that would raise the overall score most (biggest impact first)
1. **Give the world perspective and real camera angles** (all scenes, worst in s04*, s08*, s09*, s12–s17, s02b–s02e, s01a). Build sets with a vanishing point: angled walls, receding floors and 3/4 views. Use high, low and top-down angles the way the reference does (café table top-down, ping-pong overhead, stadium, street corners).
2. **Rebuild the Act 3 courtyard coverage** (s12a–s18a). Stop reusing one blurred façade behind every CU. Shoot reverse angles with different backgrounds. Add a low golden sun with god rays and long shadows that warm up as they reconcile.
3. **Fix exposure and contrast to match the reference** (global). Bring windows down to warm orange gradients, deepen the purples and blacks, and give every character a strong warm rim on the key-light side.
4. **Add acting and secondary motion** (global; motion strips s08f, s13e, s16f, s01a are near-static). Change the body pose on every phrase. Add breathing, head tilts, weight shifts, hand gestures, proper walk cycles with bob, and a slow camera drift in every shot.
5. **Make every thought readable** (s01f, s01j, s04e, s04g, s09d, s10b, s12e, s14e, s16b–d). Use bigger type, a darker opaque window backing, windows kept fully inside the safe area, and the full sentence held for at least 1 s before the cut.
6. **Rebuild the cold-open money shots** (s01d, s01g, s01i, s01k). The first 30 s decide retention, and these four shots look broken or placeholder.
7. **Turn the overload sequence into a real perspective space** (s05c, s06a, s06b, s06f). Use perspective cafeteria tables, a true camera orbit with parallax, and windows at varying depth with DOF.
8. **Lock character design to the reference** (all). Thin capsule limbs, consistent proportions per character, and kid proportions in the flashbacks (s08b, s15e).
9. **Put the "SAID / THOUGHT / MEANT" revisit cards back in the world** (s16b–s16d). Replace the generic UI panels in a purple void with the original scene backdrops, darkened, at the original framing.
10. **Fix unreadable micro-shots and staging errors** (s14b 0.67 s, s13d 0.94 s, s11d empty bed, s17f hug, s19 end card).

## Issues (most important first)
1. [s04a–s04h, s08a–s08e, s09b–s09f, s10a–s10f, s12a–s17f, s02b–s02e, s01a, s18a–s18e] PROBLEM: Nearly every set is a flat wall facing the lens: lockers, school façade, café front, street. Characters walk left and right along one line, so the film looks like a puppet theatre. The reference constantly uses 3/4 perspective, receding floors, top-downs (café table, ping-pong), low angles and angled streets (contact sheets 01, 03, 05, 08). → FIX: In the Canvas set builders, add a perspective floor plane with a vanishing point placed off-centre, angle the side walls (draw lockers as a trapezoid strip that recedes to the VP), scale characters by depth (y → scale), and add soft contact shadows. Give each scene at least 3 camera setups: 3/4 high, low hero and one top-down or OTS with a big blurred foreground.
2. [s12c, s12d, s13b–s13e, s14a–s14e, s15a–s15d, s15f, s16a, s16e, s17a–s17e] PROBLEM: About 30 shots of the climax use one identical blurred building backdrop at the same height and distance. Six close-ups in a row in s15 are near-identical waist-up frames (Noah left of centre, Leo right of centre), and Leo's frames in s15b/s15d/s15f are virtually duplicates. The emotional peak looks like the cheapest part of the film. → FIX: Make Leo's reverse look toward the tree and sky (low sun, rays) and Noah's reverse look toward the building. Alternate CU / MCU / OTS-with-foreground-shoulder / low angle. In s15d use a slow push-in to an ECU. Shift the light from cool-pink (s12) to deep gold with long shadows (s15–s17) so the grade tells the reconciliation.
3. [global, worst in s01a, s01c, s02b, s04b, s04d, s11b, s18a–s18e, s15e] PROBLEM: Exposure is blown. Windows clip to flat cream/white, sunset skies clip to white, and the pink sky is washed out. The reference keeps deep purple skies with a small bright sun, orange (not white) windows with gradients, and strong darks. Characters have only a faint rim. In the reference, a thick warm offset rim defines every silhouette (key_117s). → FIX: Cap window fill at #F2A24A→#C8642E vertical gradient with mullions. Lower the sky brightness by 25% and keep the sun as a small disc plus rays. Darken the floor and walls to the palette's deep plum. Draw the character rim as a 4–6 px warm (#F0A070) offset stroke on the key-light side of head and limbs in every shot.
4. [motion strips s01a, s08f, s13e, s16f; sheets s03c, s10g, s14e, s15*, s17b] PROBLEM: Characters are static. Across 8 frames of a shot only the mouth or eyes change, arms stay frozen in one pose, there is no camera drift, and wide shots are completely locked. The reference has constant subtle movement: camera creep, idle breathing and gesture changes. → FIX: Add a per-character idle layer (±2% body scale breathing at 0.3 Hz and head-tilt noise of ±3°). Key a new arm or hand pose for every VO phrase from timeline segs. Give walk cycles a vertical bob and contact poses. Add a 2–4% slow push or pan to every shot, eased.
5. [s01f, s01j, s04e, s04g, s09d, s10b, s12e, s14e, s16b–s16d, s06c–s06e] PROBLEM: Thought text is too small, too low-contrast or cut off. In s04e "Wait... Noah..." is clipped by the window glow. s10b "He seems really upset" is unreadable. In s09d the window sits on top of the bright window and the text vanishes. In s01j and s12e the typewriter is still at the first word when the shot ends. Thoughts are the core device of the film, so viewers must be able to read every key one. → FIX: Set the minimum key-thought font to 44 px at 1080p. Give windows a dark violet backing at 75% opacity with a 1 px light border, and clamp them inside 90% title-safe. Never place one over a bright window (auto-offset onto darker wall). Make the typewriter finish by 60% of the shot so the full line holds at least 1 s. Background "crowd" thoughts can stay small.
6. [s01d, s01g, s01i, s01k] PROBLEM: The first 30 s has several broken shots. In s01d Noah floats in mid-air above a bed made of floating rectangles. s01g "Noah's eyes" is two beige discs with tiny barista figures inside on a black void and does not read as eyes at all. s01i is an abstract brown void with a red ellipse and white triangles. In s01k the title is semi-transparent grey text overlapping the café roofline, far weaker than the reference's glowing cream title over the sky (key_036s). → FIX: In s01d, build a proper bed in perspective with Noah under a blanket who sits up (key the torso rotation). In s01g, draw an ECU of Noah's black head filling the frame with big white eyes that widen, plus a tiny cup reflection in each eye. In s01i, show the floor in perspective with the cup hitting it, coffee splash particles and shards with motion blur, and the barista's feet for scale. In s01k, place the title in the sky area above the café in bold cream (#FFF1D6) with a warm glow and a dark offset shadow, 2 lines, scale-in, fully opaque.
7. [s05c, s06a, s06b, s06f, s06g] PROBLEM: The overload set piece is conceptually strong, but s06b's overhead is a flat repeating wallpaper of coloured dots. s06a/s06f are a flat purple void with Noah pasted in the middle, and there is no orbit parallax. → FIX: Draw s06b as a real top-down cafeteria with long tables in perspective (slight tilt) and thought windows rising toward the camera with scale. For s06a/s06f, place windows at 3–4 depth layers with DOF and move the layers at different speeds for a real orbit. Keep the hard cut to black.
8. [all characters; s03d, s10c, s13e, s17d, s08b, s15e] PROBLEM: Character construction drifts from the reference. Limbs are thick sausages (the reference uses thin capsules), torso blocks change shape between shots (Noah's hoodie becomes a red trapezoid "dress" in s15a, s16e, s17d), legs in s03d sprawl at giant scale, and the age-8 flashback kids (s08b photo, s15e) are the same size and proportions as the teens. → FIX: Lock a rig per character: limb width = 0.12 × head diameter, torso as a fixed hoodie shape with visible arms at the sides, and legs always visible in MS. For kid versions, use 0.75 body scale, a bigger head ratio and shorter limbs.
9. [s16b, s16c, s16d] PROBLEM: The SAID / THOUGHT / MEANT recap is a generic UI card floating in a purple void with small text. It breaks the world and looks like a slide deck, at the very moment the theme should land emotionally. → FIX: Re-use each original shot's backdrop (hallway s10, classroom s03, locker s08), desaturated and darkened with a warm vignette. Keep the character at the original framing and let SAID appear as a subtitle, THOUGHT as the original violet window, and MEANT in warm gold handwriting that writes on. Use 40 px or larger for MEANT.
10. [s14b, s13d, s15c, s16e, s18c] PROBLEM: Several shots are too short to register. s14b "...What?" lasts 0.67 s and s13d 0.94 s, yet these are big reaction beats. → FIX: Extend s14b to ≥1.5 s (borrow from s14c) and s13d to ≥1.3 s. Hold reaction shots for at least 1.2 s after the line ends.
11. [s11d] PROBLEM: "Noah lies awake" but the bed is empty: no Noah is visible, just the date card over a blue room. → FIX: Draw Noah in bed in a top-down or side shot with eyes open and moonlight across his face, and put the THURSDAY→FRIDAY flip card above him.
12. [s17f] PROBLEM: The hug is read in a wide shot where the two figures merge into one blob under the tree, so the emotional release is lost. → FIX: Cut to an MS for the hug itself (key arms wrapping, a small back pat), then pull out to the wide. Add a warm lens flare.
13. [s19_endcard] PROBLEM: The end card is a dim maroon field with tiny figures in a row, so it ends the film on its weakest frame. → FIX: Put the question in large, glowing title type over the sunset street from s18e (Noah and Maya small in the distance), with the cast line-up larger at the bottom and a slow push-in.
14. [s01b, s01f, s01j] PROBLEM: The café interior uses a white rounded box as the coffee machine and a flat pink window with no café dressing, so it reads as a placeholder. The reference café has pendant lamps, the awning, tables and warm light. → FIX: Add pendant lamps with light cones, a counter with cups, a menu board and warm practical light. Draw the machine as a proper silver/black espresso machine shape.
15. [s15e] PROBLEM: The memory flashback is a flat pink skyline on an orange strip, indistinguishable in grade from the present day. → FIX: Give it a nostalgic grade (warm sepia, soft bloom, heavier grain, vignette, slight film-gate weave), kid proportions, a park with lollipop trees, and a 6-frame white-flash transition in and out.

## What works (keep)
- The story spine and script beats are strong and complete: the barista cold open, the "B" gag (s03e–s03g), the "Why is he smiling like that?" record scratch (s04g–s04h), the overload to silence cut (s06g), the "Then don't say ANYTHING!" overlay vanish (s10d–s10e), the letter reveal and the "answers what she SAID" payoff (s18d).
- The palette family, DOF, grain, letterbox and vignette are in the right direction. The bathroom teal (s07c, s07d) is a nicely distinct scene light.
- The thought-window design language (glassy violet panel, handwritten font, pop dots) is attractive. Keep it and only fix size, contrast and placement.
- The one-point corridor reveal of Maya (s04b, s04d) is the closest the film gets to reference-style composition. Use it as the template for perspective sets.
- The s09f grey torn "speculation" panels clearly separate fear from real thoughts. Good visual-rule clarity.
- The violet "B" travelling across the desaturated room (s03e) and the "200 THOUGHTS" counter (s06b) are good, explainer-style graphic beats.
- The narration-driven structure and act markers are clean, so the timing backbone can stay while shots are rebuilt.
