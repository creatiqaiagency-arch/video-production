# 04 — Camera & shot grammar

## Measured pacing of the reference (see `pacing_data.json`)
- 130 detected shots in 433 s → **mean 3.3 s per shot**, median 1.8 s.
- 69 % of shots < 3 s (action, inserts, reactions), 22 % between 3–8 s (dialogue, establishing), 9 % > 8 s (always with camera movement).

**House rule for our films: a new shot (or a major camera change) every 3–5 seconds on average.
Never hold one framing longer than ~8 s unless the camera is visibly moving and the moment is emotional.**

## Camera movement
- **Slow push-in** (scale 1.00 → 1.06 over the shot) is the default for any shot > 3 s.
- **Drift / truck** (horizontal 2–4 % of frame) on wides.
- **Crash-zoom** (scale ×1.6–2.5 in 4–6 frames, ease-out) for realisations/power activations.
- **Camera shake** (2–8 px, decaying) on impacts.
- Parallax: foreground layer moves 1.5–2× background speed.

## Framing rules
- Rule of thirds; eyes on the upper third line in close-ups.
- Look-room: characters face into the open side of the frame.
- Keep screen direction consistent within a scene (180° rule) — in our intersection, the truck always travels **right → left**.
- Match-cut by shape or motion when changing location (e.g., falling pot → table-tennis ball).

## Cutting
- Cut on action (mid-movement) or on a VO beat.
- Reaction shot after every surprising event.
- Wide → close → reaction → wide is the base rhythm; vary it.
- Use 0.3–0.5 s holds before punchlines.
