# 11 — Story structure (from the reference breakdown)

## Why the format works
- **Title = broad counterfactual about YOU**: "What if" + "your" + familiar ability + extraordinary
  change. The viewer supplies their own examples.
- **The title sells the fantasy; the story explores the consequences.**
- Two levels of curiosity: Level A "what can the ability do?" → Level B "what happens to the
  person who has it?" Level B sustains 5–7 minutes.

## Narrative engine
1. Give the character an extraordinary difference.
2. Show it in ordinary, recognisable situations (proof within seconds).
3. Move toward a personally significant goal.
4. Increase the scale of the challenge / add consequences.
5. Return to the character's life — what did it change?

## Reference macro-structure (7:13)
0:00–0:32 character + human situation → 0:32 title card → 0:32–2:16 establish ability with
everyday proof → 2:16–3:52 measurable performance (numbers, sports) → 3:52–5:12 consequences
through relationships → 5:12–6:56 big stakes → 6:56–7:13 return to everyday + CTA.

## Attention devices to reuse
- Cold open with a physical event proving the premise (falling cup).
- **Re-hook questions** every 30–60 s (new question replaces the answered one).
- Anticipate and kill viewer objections on screen (headphones, bathroom).
- Visual proof inserts (numbers, labels) at the moment of doubt.
- Contrast through progression: fast/playful → crowded/uncomfortable → quiet/sincere.

## Story auditor checklist
- Is the core question clear in the first 15 s?
- Does every scene raise or answer a question?
- Is cause → effect visible (not just narrated)?
- Do we always know whose thought we're hearing and whether it's real or imagined?
- Does the ending demonstrate the lesson through action rather than speech?
