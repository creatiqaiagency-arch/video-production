# Story and attention principles (condensed from the Ifverse breakdown)

1. **Broad hypothetical, specific story.** The title sells a universal fantasy ("What if YOUR ___ was 10× ___?"). The video keeps viewers through one character's specific story.
2. **Two curiosity loops.** (A) What can the ability do? (B) What happens to the person who has it? Alternate between them.
3. **Show the power before explaining it.** The hook demonstrates within 6 s and the title card lands around 30–40 s (the reference puts its title card at 0:32).
4. **One clearly defined fictional rule** plus explicit limits, so it doesn't become "anything goes".
5. **Measurable proof.** Use numbers, timers and before/after comparisons. Abstract ability becomes concrete.
6. **Vary the viewer's mental task:** read a face → predict an outcome → compare numbers → feel a moment.
7. **Escalation.** Move from a personal test, to a public status change, to a competition with stakes.
8. **Plant and payoff.** Anything that solves the climax must be seen earlier.
9. **Emotional beats breathe.** Show absence (an empty chair or workstation) rather than narrating it.
10. **Return to the ordinary world at the end.** Use a callback to the opening image.
11. **Objects as transitions.** Piano key → keyboard key → textbook → football field; test F → test A+.
12. **Character design economy.** Simple figures and rich environments mean the budget goes to light and staging.
